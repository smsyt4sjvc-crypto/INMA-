The reality

You don't know who you're really hiring.

The exterior remodeling industry has a trust problem. Here's what most homeowners don't find out until it's too late.

Two bad options. Until now.

Option A — the national brand

Hire a national company. They spend millions on ads and sales commissions, then subcontract the work to the same local crews you could hire direct. You're paying 30–40% for their overhead — not for better windows, better siding, or better installers.

Option B — the $200 license

Hire the cheapest guy off Craigslist, Facebook, or HomeAdvisor. In Washington a contractor's license costs a couple hundred bucks. That's enough to start a business — not enough to know how to run one. Every installer who sees his boss charge $5,000 and pay him $1,800 thinks he should go out on his own. Pure intent, but no understanding of overhead, margin, or operating capital. He starts your job, runs out of cash halfway through, and either asks for more money or disappears chasing a cash gig to make payroll. You get stuck.

That's why five bids on the same job come back at $12k, $14k, $17k, $20k, $22k and you have no idea why. That's the gap I fill.

Unlicensed contractors operate openly — they just don't advertise it. If they damage your home, you have no recourse.

National companies mark up identical products 30–40% to cover corporate overhead, national ad buys, and sales commissions.

No one is verifying insurance, bond status, or work history for you — unless you hire someone whose job it is to do exactly that.

When problems come up mid-project, you want someone with field experience in your corner — on the phone and on the walk — not a call center reading a script.

The INMA model

You hire me. I represent you.

Think of INMA like a buyer's agent — but for remodeling. I don't work for the contractor. I work for you. My job is to look out for your money and your interests from scope to closeout.

I walk your project.

I come out as an advocate, not a bidder. We walk the project together and I build a real scope — line by line. No vague "per contract" language. No "that's not included" surprises later.

We set your budget. My fee comes out in front of you.

Using my published Fair Market Report — every rate sourced from public cost data, so you can verify the math yourself — we map out what your project should cost. You decide your budget. My retainer (typically 10–15% depending on scope complexity) comes out of that number dollar-for-dollar: on a $30,000 budget at 10%, my fee is $3,000, and the offer that reaches the contractor is $27,000. You watch it happen. I'm not marking anything up.

I present your offer to a vetted crew.

Same play as a buyer's agent submitting an offer on a house. I take your fair-market offer — with the full scope and material takeoff attached — to one of the licensed local crews I've personally vetted. They either accept your terms or they pass. If they accept, you sign their installation contract directly with them at that number, and your material deposit goes straight to them. I never touch your construction money.

Electric is up 94%. Gas is projected up 114% by 2030. That's not a prediction — it's already happening. The smartest move isn't arguing your utility bill — it's using less energy.

The biggest source of wasted energy in most Spokane homes isn't your furnace. It's your envelope — old windows, aluminum frames, and uninsulated walls bleeding heat every hour of every winter night.

A single-pane window loses heat 75% faster than a modern ENERGY STAR vinyl double-pane. Aluminum frames are worse — aluminum conducts heat over 1,000× faster than vinyl. If you have frost or condensation pooling on your sills in January, your frames are near exterior temperature inside your house. You're paying for that every month.

New vinyl windows with low-E glass and argon fill cut that loss dramatically. Avista pays up to $225 per window in rebates on contractor-installed ENERGY STAR units — and their Energy Smart Loan lets you finance the rest at 7.5% APR with zero down, paid back on your monthly Avista bill.

Quick window test

Hold a lighter or flashlight up to your window glass. You should see 3 or 4 reflections. If one has a metallic green or pink tint, that's your low-E coating working. If they all look the same, your windows don't have low-E and you're losing energy every day.
