01Direction

The one line

I'm not a contractor. I'm your personal homeowner advocate. I put your project in writing, priced to a fair market you can check yourself, and you take it to any contractor you choose.

The problem

A homeowner re-sides a house once, maybe twice. Contractors sell every day. That gap is why homeowners get burned: by the national-brand salesman who can't explain why one product beats another, and by the new local installer with a $200 license and no business experience. INMA puts a repeat player on the homeowner's side of the table, before the mistake instead of after it.

Where the company is going

Before (retired)

  • Buyer's agent with a network of vetted crews
  • 10–15% fee carved out of the contract
  • Matching homeowners to contractors
  • Coordinating through the job

Now

  • Standalone products, each priced on its own
  • No percentage of the project, no fee in the contract
  • The homeowner picks the contractor, any contractor
  • INMA never directs, supervises, or pays for construction

What I'm building

  • The Offer Sheet as the standard. A written offer from the homeowner to the contractor: scope, installation terms set to code and the manufacturer's written instructions, a fair-market price, and payment terms that protect the homeowner. The contractor accepts it as written or passes.
  • Published, checkable pricing. The Spokane Fair Market Report, kept current with weekly permit pulls and pricing updates, so every number on an Offer Sheet traces to a public source. The ranges set the market; the Offer Sheet closes the gap to the homeowner's number, because a range doesn't know their house.
  • Both ends of the job. The Offer Sheet before the work starts, and a Completion Review after, so the homeowner knows it's right before paying the balance.
  • Leverage, hindsight ahead of time. Twenty-five years of trade experience, applied before the homeowner signs anything.

Lines INMA doesn't cross

  • Not a contractor, general contractor, or construction manager. Never hires, directs, supervises, or guarantees the work.
  • Never collects, holds, or pays out construction money. No cut of the contract, nothing paid by the crew.
  • Not a home inspector. The Completion Review checks installed work against the Offer Sheet and the manufacturer's instructions, never the condition of the house.
  • No contractor network, no "vetted crews," no matching.

Anatomy of an Offer Sheet

To the contractor

Written homeowner to contractor, in "I" and "you." Nothing on it that isn't the contractor's business.

  1. Photos, job header, applicable code, installation terms
  2. Job specifics and scope of work
  3. The offer, change orders, paperwork, payment terms, warranty
  4. Agreement (governs over any other contract's payment terms) and signatures
  5. Installation guidance (manufacturer summary)
  6. Material order: items and quantities, never material prices

Initials on every page.

To the homeowner

"Your Offer Sheet: How to use it." Kept by the homeowner, never handed to the contractor.

  • What the sheet is and that they own it
  • Ten steps from reading it to paying the balance
  • What to watch for: second contracts, "substantial completion," draws, a crew that disappears
  • Why not the lowest price
  • How the price was set, tied to the Fair Market Report
  • Their home's conditions, explained
  • Recommended use within 120 days; no expiration

Standard terms

Up to 50% down, nothing more until complete and accepted · changes by written change order only · 3 consecutive working days absent without notice ends the agreement · 30 days to correct written deficiencies · 5-year workmanship warranty · extra material billed at the offer rate, no pickup or delivery charges · permits and tax added by receipt.

03Voice

How INMA sounds

Plainspoken, confident, honest about tradeoffs. A trusted local tradesman, not a tech startup and not a salesman. Short sentences. Real numbers. No hype, no exclamation points.

Say this

  • "I'm not a contractor. I'm your personal homeowner advocate."
  • "You make the offer. They accept your terms or they pass."
  • "It's yours. Take it to any contractor you choose."
  • "The numbers online don't know your house. I close that gap."
  • "Fair-market pricing you can check yourself."
  • "Code is the legal minimum, not a standard of quality."
  • "The cheapest bid is rarely where the most value lives."
  • "If you look up what it costs..." when pointing to public pricing.
  • "Know it's right before you pay the balance."
  • "Hire the right guy, the first time."

Never say this

  • Anything that implies INMA controls or answers for the work: "we handle the scope and accountability," "we manage the job."
  • "Vetted crews," "our network," "we'll match you."
  • Any fee taken from or deducted out of the contract.
  • "Inspection" or "inspector" for INMA's own services.
  • "Guaranteed savings" or "we'll beat any quote."
  • "Ask AI." Say "if you look up."
  • A bid. An Offer Sheet is an estimate of fair-market cost and the homeowner's offer.
  • Seasonal-only language for the Tune-Up. It's year-round.

Writing rules

  • No em dashes in posts, ads, or copy. Use a period or a comma.
  • Offer Sheets speak homeowner to contractor ("Keep siding 6" above finished grade."). Homeowner explanations go in the guide.
  • Social posts: concise. Lead with the problem the homeowner feels, then the fix, then the link and phone number.
  • Numbers over adjectives. "$19.00 per square foot, the midpoint of the published range," not "competitive pricing."

Standard disclosure

INMA is not a contractor and does not hire, direct, or supervise any work. INMA provides project scoping, fair-market pricing, and homeowner advocacy. An Offer Sheet is an estimate of fair-market cost for the scope described, not a bid.

05Applying it

The website

  • Backdrop: the waterfall art (climbing figure) fixed behind every public page.
  • Layout: dark and light sections alternating, hairline rules, numbered section eyebrows ("05 / Standalone products").
  • Motion: none for show. No scroll reveals. The bottom contact bar stays fixed.
  • Photo: Jake's personal photo stays in full color everywhere. Everything else monochrome.
  • Logo: the hand-painted white bear on red, square with small rounded corners. Never recolored.

Documents (Offer Sheets, guides, pricing, agreements)

  • White letter-size sheet, ink #111, body in Helvetica/Arial for clean print. On screen, the sheet sits on black with Print and Download buttons that hide when printed.
  • Letterhead: bear (48–64px) + INMA wordmark + tagline left, document title right in spaced caps, 3px black rule under it.
  • Section headers: solid black bars, white spaced caps. Tables: black header row, light gray hairlines, numbers right-aligned.
  • Every page ends with the footer. Agreements carry initials on every page and signature lines on the agreement page.
  • Never force a field. Every document can print blank and be filled by hand.
