# GLP-1 wardrobe-replacement cycle

Jake's question (2026-07-30): *"What cities have the most GLP-1s been prescribed, what clothing retailers are
dominant there, and when do the schools start?"* — i.e. **geographically concentrated wardrobe-replacement
demand, timed to the back-to-school apparel window.**

Links: [[consumption-vs-investment-crux]], [[retail-edge]], [[trade-down-landing-pads]], [[quiet-health-screen]]

## DATA (observed)

### GLP-1 geography — and there are TWO DIFFERENT DISTRIBUTIONS
- **ALL GLP-1 fills, per 1,000 people (state):** **Kentucky 21.0 · West Virginia 18.9 · Alaska 17.5 ·
  Mississippi 16.1 · Louisiana 15.4.** (Newsweek/Fox citing prescription data.)
- **City:** **Bowling Green, Kentucky is reported as the US "Ozempic capital."** Population **~75,000**.
- **Brooklyn and Miami: ~1% of residents (≈10 per 1,000)** — well below Kentucky's 21.
- **⭑ WEIGHT-LOSS-SPECIFIC fills (Wegovy, Zepbound, Saxenda, Qsymia, phentermine) are a DIFFERENT map:
  highest in MASSACHUSETTS, NEW JERSEY, DELAWARE, NEW HAMPSHIRE; VERMONT has the highest combined
  weight-loss fill rate in the US.** (GoodRx fill tracker / Health Management Academy.)

### School start dates, 2026-27
- **Jackson, Mississippi: July 29** — among the earliest in the nation (i.e. yesterday).
- **Florida: 38 of 67 counties start Monday August 10**, the earliest date state law allows.
- **Texas: major districts August 12** (Lake Travis, Coppell, Tyler, Aubrey ISDs).
- **New York: September 10. Labor Day 2026 = September 7.** Northeast broadly post-Labor-Day.
- **National modal window: August 15–20.**

### Retail geography (⚠️ structural knowledge, NOT store-count data — verify before use)
- **Belk** (~290 stores, almost entirely Southeast) is the regional department store of the high-all-GLP-1
  states. **Private (Sycamore), Chapter 11 in 2021 — not investable and financially stressed.**
- **Academy Sports (ASO)** Texas/Southeast-concentrated; **Ross (ROST)** West/South-heavy; **Burlington (BURL)**
  more Northeast/urban; **TJX** national. **Off-price over-indexes to the South.**

## THESIS (interpretation — NOT fact)

- *(⛔ THE THESIS WANTS THE WRONG MAP — this is the finding)* **The apparel signal requires WEIGHT-LOSS-indication
  prescribing, not total GLP-1 volume.** A type-2 diabetic on Ozempic may lose modest weight; someone on
  Wegovy/Zepbound for obesity loses **15–20% of body weight = 2–4 sizes** and must replace a wardrobe.
  **Total-fill geography is KY/WV/MS/LA. Weight-loss-fill geography is MA/NJ/DE/NH/VT.** **The intuitive answer
  — the South — is the wrong distribution for this trade.**
- *(⛔ AND THE TWO VARIABLES ARE ANTI-CORRELATED)* **The high weight-loss-prescribing states are the LATEST
  school starts (Northeast, post-Labor-Day, ~Sept 7–10). The earliest starts (Jackson MS Jul 29, Florida Aug 10,
  Texas Aug 12) sit in the high-diabetes / lower-weight-loss states.** **Where the wardrobe demand is, school
  starts last. Where school starts first, the wardrobe demand is weakest.** The overlay the question was
  looking for runs the wrong way.
- *(⛔ THE STRUCTURAL PROBLEM, and it is larger than the geography)* **Back-to-school apparel is bought for
  CHILDREN. GLP-1 use is adult.** **Two different populations.** The school calendar dates *kids'* apparel
  purchases and says nothing about when a 45-year-old who lost 40 lb buys trousers. **A basket effect (parent
  shopping for kids also buys for self) is real but second-order — and it does not need the GLP-1 overlay to
  work.**
- *(⚠️ THE CONFOUND)* **GLP-1 prevalence, retail mix and school calendars all correlate with region and income.**
  A "GLP-1 city × dominant retailer" screen may simply be **rediscovering that the South is poorer and shops
  off-price.** Any result must be tested against a plain region/income control.
- *(⚠️ SCALE)* **Bowling Green is ~75,000 people.** A city-level GLP-1 ranking at that scale is **noise for a
  national retailer's comps.** City granularity is the wrong resolution for the question.

### ★★★ WHAT SURVIVES — and it is the better version of the idea, without geography
- **TRANSITIONAL SIZING.** A user losing 15–20% over 12–18 months **passes THROUGH 2–4 sizes**. They need
  clothes they will wear for a few months, not for years. **That structurally favours OFF-PRICE and VALUE over
  full-price and premium** — Ross, TJX/Marshalls, Burlington, Old Navy (Gap), Walmart, Target — **and it
  favours RESALE** (ThredUp is the listed pure-play; Poshmark private; The RealReal). **It is a headwind for
  anything sold on fit-investment and durability: premium denim, tailored clothing.**
- **★★★ AND THE RIGHT TIMING VARIABLE IS NOT THE SCHOOL CALENDAR — it is the NEW-START CURVE, LAGGED.**
  Wardrobe replacement happens roughly **4–8 months after initiation**, once enough weight is off to force it.
  **So apparel demand is a lagged function of GLP-1 STARTS, not of August.** **To time it, take the GLP-1
  new-start series (GoodRx fill tracker, IQVIA) and lag it two quarters.** That is a real, published,
  higher-frequency series and it answers "when" properly.
- *(⚠️ novelty check)* **The GLP-1 apparel-replacement thesis has been in sell-side notes since 2023** (Walmart
  flagged a GLP-1 food-basket effect in Oct 2023). **The idea is not new; the only thing that could be new is
  the lagged-start timing, and that is the piece worth building.**
